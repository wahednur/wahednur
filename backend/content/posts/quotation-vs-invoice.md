Both documents list work and prices, so they look alike. They do very different jobs.

| | Quotation | Invoice |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | An offer: "this is what it would cost" | A bill: "this is what you owe" |
| When | Before work starts | When payment is due |
| Binding? | Only once you accept it | Yes, for the agreed amount |
| Can it expire? | Yes, it has a validity date | It has a due date instead |

## How they connect in my workflow

1. I send a **quotation**.
2. You **accept** it (or decline, or ask for changes).
3. The accepted quotation is **converted into an invoice** with the same lines and total.
4. The invoice is **issued** and, from then on, its lines cannot be edited. This keeps the record honest for both of us.
5. Payments are recorded against the invoice until it is fully paid.

The point of converting instead of retyping is simple: the invoice can never quietly differ from what you accepted.

## A rule I keep

An invoice stays open until it is fully paid. A partial payment is recorded as a partial payment, not rounded away, and the remaining amount stays visible to you and to me.
