My path was not a straight line. Each step left something useful.

## 2010 to 2019: visual effects, animation and graphics
I learned to present an idea so another person understands it quickly. In software this shows up as screens that are clear before they are clever, and as working walkthroughs you can react to before the real build, like the demo I made for a school.

## 2014 to 2016: websites for NGO and development-sector clients
First client web work, with WordPress and Joomla in Dhaka. I learned how real clients write requirements, change their minds and need things explained in plain language. That is why I write scopes in plain language.

## 2020 to 2021: defining an eCommerce core system
For a project in Saudi Arabia I defined the business logic and features and directed development. I did not write that code. It taught me how to turn business needs into a specification a developer can follow, which is what a good written scope is.

## 2024 to 2025: MERN training, then Django and Next.js
I completed formal MERN training and started building full applications. I now use Django REST Framework with PostgreSQL for the backend and Next.js or React for the front end.

## Running my own store and a repair-business system
- **ekhaneikini.com** (my own online store, since 2016): stock as a ledger of movements, courier handling, Cash on Delivery and online payment, permission-based staff access, an offline-first mobile order app.
- **A service and parts management system**: one open invoice per customer, payments in parts, due tracking, supplier and customer ledgers.

## What this means for you
- If you sell products, I already know the shop side: variations, stock, returns, delivery, local payment habits.
- If your customers pay in parts, I have built invoices and ledgers around exactly that.
- If you are not technical, I explain the "why" before the "how".

## What it does not mean
It does not mean I have done everything. If your project is far from these areas, I will say so before you pay anything.
