I built my first client websites with WordPress and Joomla, so this is not a comparison from the outside.

## Where WordPress is the right answer
- A company site, a portfolio or a blog with standard needs.
- A small budget and a short timeline.
- You want to edit pages yourself with a familiar editor.
- A plugin already does what you need.

In those cases, building custom software would waste your money.

## Where it starts to hurt
- **Unusual business rules.** Payment in parts, special order flows, stock as a ledger, role-based work screens. Plugins stretch, then break or conflict.
- **Many plugins.** Each is another thing to update and another way in for an attacker. Updates can clash.
- **Performance and structure.** A heavy theme plus many plugins can slow a site down.
- **Data you need to trust.** Reports built on top of plugin tables are hard to keep consistent.

## Where custom development pays
- Your process is the product: ordering, invoices with installments, subscriptions, a client area.
- You need permissions that match your team.
- You want a clear data model you own.

## Where custom development costs you
- A higher starting cost and a longer wait.
- Every feature is built, tested and maintained by someone. There is no plugin to install.
- You depend on whoever knows the code, which is why handover and documentation should be agreed in the scope.

## How I decide with a client
I ask what the site must do that a ready-made tool cannot. If the answer is "nothing", I tell you to use a ready-made tool.
