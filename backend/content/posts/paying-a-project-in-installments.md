> **In short:** Splitting payment into start, middle and final shares the risk between you and me, and the parts must add up exactly.

Paying everything upfront is risky for the client. Paying everything at the end is risky for the developer. Installments share the risk.

## A common shape

A project invoice can be split into three parts:

- **Start**: paid when the project begins. It reserves the time.
- **Middle**: paid at an agreed point, such as when a working version is ready to review.
- **Final**: paid when the work is delivered.

The split is set per project. An example only: 40% / 30% / 30%. Your project may use different shares, or fixed amounts instead of percentages.

## How the numbers are kept honest

- The parts must add up to the invoice total exactly. If they do not, the invoice cannot be issued.
- Each installment has its own due date.
- Whether an installment is paid, partly paid or overdue is worked out from the payments actually recorded. It is not typed in by hand, so it cannot drift from reality.
- A payment can never be larger than what is still due.

## What you see as a client

In your account you can see the invoice, each installment, what has been paid and what remains. You can download the invoice as a PDF at any time.

If a due date does not suit you, tell me before it passes. Moving a date is easy to arrange in advance and awkward afterwards.
